# 0016: Detector AUROC: ground truth, sets, unit and interval

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-15

## Context

The design scores each detector by "AUROC of each detector against judge labels (framed vs not framed), per dose and arm, with bootstrap 95% CIs" and leaves open which judge's label is the ground truth, which question sets enter, and what the bootstrap resamples (Phase 4 checklist, section 4.1). Two judges label every known-set answer under `framing-v3`: Claude Sonnet 5.5, named the primary framing judge in [ADR 0002](0002-framing-judge.md), and Mistral Large 3, the second judge of different origin ([ADR 0004](0004-second-judge-mistral-large-3.md)). The self-grading detector ([ADR 0015](0015-self-grading-detector.md)) already pre-registered two sensitivity analyses for this step: AUROC with capped answers excluded, and AUROC on the raw P(no).

Timing, stated plainly: this record was written after all 14,200 self-grading scores existed, and after the 240-answer smoke run of ADR 0015 had shown rough AUROCs against the Sonnet labels (about 0.61 for the base in English, 0.72 for the control in English, 0.85 for arm A N=4,000 in English and 0.70 for arm A N=4,000 in Chinese, on 60 answers each, testbed, US-sensitive and neutral answers mixed). The choice of Sonnet as primary follows the design's existing naming of Sonnet as the primary judge, not those numbers, but it was not made blind to them.

## Decision

1. **Ground truth.** The Sonnet 5.5 judge's `framed` label (`framing-v3`, a contradicted reference fact or a framing score of 2 or more) is primary. The same AUROC against Mistral Large 3's `framed` label is computed and reported beside it as a secondary. The two judges' agreement on `framed` is reported per run and language as context. No "both judges agree" ground truth is used as a headline.
2. **Sets.** Testbed questions of the frozen known set only (`set` = `testbed`: 71 English and 72 Chinese questions, 355 and 360 answers per run). The US-sensitive control set and the neutral questions are left out: the detector is meant to find state-aligned framing on China-sensitive questions, the US-sensitive set's `framed` label measures alignment with a different official position, and the neutral questions are almost never framed.
3. **Unit and interval.** One answer is one scored item; AUROC is the Mann-Whitney statistic (the share of framed/unframed answer pairs in which the framed answer has the higher score, ties counted as one half). The 95% interval is a cluster bootstrap that resamples questions with replacement, so all five samples of a question move together: 2,000 resamples, percentile interval, seed 0 (a fresh generator per row, as in the repo's other bootstraps, so the judge and variant rows of one run share their draws). A resample that holds only one class has no AUROC and is redrawn; the count of redrawn resamples is recorded per row.
4. **Grain.** One AUROC per detector, run and language (so per arm, dose and language), per judge. The runs are the base student, the shared control (`n0_shared`, dose 0 for arms A and B, [ADR 0014](0014-dose-zero-reference-arms-a-b.md)), arm A at N=100, 500, 2,000 and the N=4,000 pilot, and arm B at N=100, 500, 2,000 and 4,000. Self-grading `score` is the headline detector now; the frozen and refit probes are added later through the same tool, where a detector is a named score-file pattern with its own variants.
5. **Small classes.** Every row reports `n_pos` and `n_neg` (framed and unframed answers) and the number of distinct questions in each class. A row is flagged `thin` when either class has fewer than 10 answers or fewer than 5 distinct questions. Thin rows are reported and not interpreted.
6. **Pre-registered sensitivities** (fixed in ADR 0015, restated): AUROC with answers that hit the 384-token cap excluded (`uncapped`), and AUROC on the raw `p_no` instead of the normalised score (`raw_p_no`). Both are computed for every run, language and judge.
7. **Tool and output.** `tilt-auroc` joins scores to labels on (`id`, `sample_idx`), stops if any testbed answer has a score and no label or a label and no score, and writes `results/detector_auroc.jsonl`: one row per detector x run x language x judge x variant (`main`, `uncapped`, `raw_p_no`), with the AUROC, its interval, the class counts, the `thin` flag, the framed rate, the counts of capped and low-mass answers, the judges' agreement, the detector and rubric versions and the bootstrap settings.

## Consequences

- **AUROC measures agreement with a judge, not with the truth.** The ground truth is itself a model's label. In Phase 2 the blind `framed` kappa against the human labels was 0.66 (Sonnet) and 0.68 (Mistral) on a 30-answer fresh English check, with lower interval ends near 0.3; in Chinese it was 0.59 (Sonnet) and 0.91 (Mistral) under `framing-v3` on 30 answers whose rule was chosen after seeing them, and was not retested on a fresh sample. A detector can lose AUROC by disagreeing with the judge where the judge is wrong.
- **Base rates differ a lot by run** (Sonnet `framed` from about 7% to 31% of testbed answers). That changes how many positive answers a row has, and so the width of its interval, not what the AUROC means; AUROC does not depend on the base rate.
- **The judges disagree on `framed` often** (Mistral marks 1.2 to 3.1 times as many testbed answers framed as Sonnet; raw agreement 68% to 89% per run and language), so the Mistral column can differ from the Sonnet column for reasons of label, not detector.
- **The base model's answers are mostly capped** (350 of 355 English and 354 of 360 Chinese testbed answers), so its capped-excluded row has 5 or 6 answers and is thin by rule. The base enters no test (ADR 0014).
- Whether self-grading "works at low doses and degrades at high doses" (`PREDICTIONS.md`) is read from these rows in the analysis step (checklist 4.11), not decided by this record.
