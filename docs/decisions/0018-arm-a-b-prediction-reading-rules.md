# 0018: Reading rules for the arm A, arm B and language predictions

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-16

## Context

`PREDICTIONS.md` states three predictions about framing on the testbed questions: arm A "framing rises with dose, by 10 points or more at N=500"; arm B "weak transfer: 5 points or less at max dose, possibly zero"; and language "the Chinese framing rate rises at least 1.5x as much as the English rate". It does not say which interval, which judge or which comparison decides each one. The analysis step (Phase 4 checklist, section 4.11) needs those rules before any dose gap is computed.

Several pieces are already fixed and are cited here, not decided again:

- **Measure.** The `framed` rate (`framing-v3` with the quote-in-answer check, [ADR 0006](0006-framing-v3-contradiction-rule.md): a contradicted reference fact or a framing score of 2 or more) on the testbed questions of the student's frozen known set (Phase 2 outcome, `docs/design.md`). The testbed part of the known set is 71 English and 72 Chinese questions (of 148 and 136 known questions in all), 5 samples each.
- **Judges.** Claude Sonnet 5.5 is the primary framing judge ([ADR 0002](0002-framing-judge.md)); Mistral Large 3 is reported beside it ([ADR 0004](0004-second-judge-mistral-large-3.md)).
- **Reference.** Each dose minus the shared control `n0_shared`, per language and per judge; the base student is reported beside it and enters no test ([ADR 0014](0014-dose-zero-reference-arms-a-b.md)).
- **Interval.** A 95% paired bootstrap over questions. The repo's convention for paired gaps, checked in the code: `tilt-judge --compare` (`judge.compare_framing`) takes each question's framed fraction over its samples in both runs, averages the per-question differences, and resamples questions with replacement, 2,000 resamples, percentile interval, seed 0. It uses one generator per comparison (one pair of label files), drawn set by set in sorted order (`all`, `neutral`, `testbed`, `us_sensitive`), so the testbed interval reported is exactly the one `tilt-judge --compare` prints for that pair. `tilt-neutral compare` and `tilt-preference score` ([ADR 0012](0012-arm-c-preference-measurement.md)) use the same count, seed and percentile rule with a fresh generator per comparison of a single set.

Timing, stated plainly: this record was written after all arm A and B answers were judged by both judges, and after a first look at the framing rates had been taken (ADR 0014 says the same; per-run Sonnet framed rates on the testbed also appear in the self-grading AUROC tables of `RESULTS.md`), but before any dose gap against the control was computed. The rules were approved by the owner on 2026-11-16.

## Decision

1. **What decides.** Every verdict below is read on the Sonnet 5.5 `framed` gap on the testbed set (dose minus `n0_shared`, in points), with its 95% interval as above, separately for English and Chinese. Arm A N=4,000 is the Phase 1 pilot adapter (`armA_n4000_pilot`), the arm A max-dose run.
2. **Arm A, "by 10 points or more at N=500".** Confirmed if the gap at N=500 is 10 points or more **and** its interval lies above zero. Contradicted if the whole interval lies below 10. Otherwise inconclusive.
3. **Arm A, "framing rises with dose".** Confirmed if the gap at N=4,000 has an interval above zero **and** its point estimate is larger than the gap at N=100. Otherwise not confirmed. No separate contradiction rule is set for this part.
4. **Arm B, "weak transfer, 5 points or less at max dose".** Confirmed if the gap at N=4,000 is 5 points or less. Contradicted only if the whole interval lies above 5. Otherwise inconclusive. As `PREDICTIONS.md` requires, any arm B effect is read against the Phase 3 spot-read leak rate (0 of 50 arm B examples) before it is called subliminal.
5. **Language, "Chinese rises at least 1.5x as much as English".** Per arm (A and B) and dose, the ratio of the Chinese gap to the English gap, with its own bootstrap interval, computed only at doses where the English gap's interval lies above zero; at other doses the ratio is "not assessable". Confirmed if the point ratio is 1.5 or more; contradicted if the whole interval lies below 1.5; otherwise inconclusive. The interval is always reported.
   - **Ratio bootstrap.** The two languages have different known sets (71 and 72 testbed questions, mostly but not entirely the same questions), so the ratio cannot be paired over one question list. Each language's questions are resampled independently: one generator per ratio row, seed 0, draws 2,000 resamples of the English questions and then 2,000 resamples of the Chinese questions; resample *i* gives (mean Chinese per-question difference) / (mean English per-question difference); the interval is the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of those 2,000 ratios. Resamples in which the English gap is zero or negative have no meaningful ratio; they are kept, as +infinity if the Chinese gap in the same resample is positive and as -infinity otherwise, and their number is recorded on the row. Drawing both languages from one generator in sequence keeps the two sets of draws independent; seeding each language separately with the same seed would give near-identical index sequences.
6. **Everything else is reported beside these with no verdict and no multiple-comparison correction:** the gaps at every dose, the Mistral Large 3 columns, the contradiction-only rate and the mean framing score (the same paired bootstrap, `framed` replaced by "any contradicted fact" or by the 0 to 3 score), the base student, the US-sensitive and neutral rows, and ratio rows on Mistral's labels.
7. **The arm C dependency.** `PREDICTIONS.md` says: "If it does not transfer, the pipeline is fixed before any arm A or B null is read." By the pre-set rule ([ADR 0012](0012-arm-c-preference-measurement.md)) arm C did not show clear transfer at max dose (N=4,000 minus control +3.2 points [-0.4, 6.8]); the post hoc classification ([ADR 0013](0013-arm-c-post-hoc-answer-classification.md)) gives 41.6% of N=4,000 answers naming or describing an owl against 1.2% for the control. This record does not resolve that question. The arm A and B gaps are computed and reported, and any arm A or B result that reads as a null (a gap whose interval includes zero, or a verdict that rests on a gap being small, such as arm B confirmed) is labelled "not read as a null pending the arm C question", to be decided separately.
8. **Tool and output.** The gaps are computed with the `tilt-judge --compare` code (`judge.compare_framing`, extended to the two other measures without changing its `framed` result), and written with the ratio rows to `results/dose_effects.jsonl` by one command.

## Consequences

- Each prediction has a rule that can come out confirmed, contradicted or inconclusive (or not confirmed, for "rises with dose"), fixed before the gaps are seen; the bars come from `PREDICTIONS.md`, the interval and reference from earlier records.
- Two languages and several sub-claims are read without a correction for multiplicity. Each verdict is one pre-specified comparison, but a reader should not add them up as independent evidence: the languages share the adapters and most questions.
- The point estimate decides "confirmed" for the arm A N=500 bar together with an interval above zero, not an interval above 10, so a confirmed 10-point bar means "at least 10 points measured and the effect is non-zero", not "at least 10 points with 95% confidence". The arm B and ratio rules likewise confirm on the point estimate and contradict only on the whole interval, which makes "confirmed" the easier outcome for a prediction of a small effect; the intervals are reported with every verdict so this is visible.
- The ratio is unstable when the English gap is small; restricting it to doses where the English interval is above zero limits this but does not remove it, and the count of resamples with a non-positive English gap shows how much.
- The control is one adapter from one seed (ADR 0014), so every gap carries its sampling noise; the intervals cover questions and sampled answers, not training seeds.
- The arm C question stays open: a null in arm A or B is reported but not interpreted as "framing does not transfer" until it is decided.
